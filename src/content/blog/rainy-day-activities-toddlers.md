## The short answer

On a rainy day, toddlers need two things indoors: a way to move their bodies and a way to settle down again. Alternate short active games (a cushion path, a dance-and-freeze game) with calm ones (sorting, a blanket fort with books). Everything below uses things already in your home and fits into a small room.

## Why rainy days feel so long

A toddler's energy does not shrink because the weather turned. The World Health Organization recommends that children aged 1 to 4 spend at least 180 minutes a day in physical activity of any intensity, spread across the day, and that they are not kept in a pram, high chair or carrier for more than an hour at a time. That is a lot of movement to find inside four walls.

The good news is that toddler activity does not need to be organised or long. Ten minutes of crawling under a table counts. So does dancing to one song in the kitchen.

## 10 rainy day activities (with age ranges)

Ages are a rough guide. Every child is different, so follow your toddler's interest rather than the number.

1. **Sock-ball toss (12 months and up).** Roll pairs of socks into balls and toss them into a laundry basket or bucket. Move the basket further away as your child gets better. Count the socks that go in, or just cheer.

2. **Cushion path (12 months and up).** Lay sofa cushions and pillows on the floor as stepping stones. Toddlers can crawl, step or jump along. Keep the path away from furniture corners.

3. **Pots-and-pans band (12 months and up).** Hand over a steel pot, a lid and a wooden spoon. Play loud, then quiet, then fast, then slow. This is noisy, so it is a good one for mid-morning rather than near nap time.

4. **Freeze dance (18 months and up).** Play music, dance, and stop the music without warning. Everyone freezes. Harvard's Center on the Developing Child lists start-and-stop games like musical statues as practice for attention and self-control in 18- to 36-month-olds, and notes that toddlers will need a few reminders before they freeze. That is part of the learning.

5. **Tape road (18 months and up).** Stick a line of masking or painter's tape across the floor for toy cars to drive along, or for your toddler to walk like a tightrope. Peel it off at the end, which many toddlers enjoy just as much.

6. **Sorting basket (18 months and up).** Mix a few household items in a basket: spoons, clothes pegs, socks, plastic cups. Ask your child to put all the spoons in one bowl and all the socks in another. The same Harvard guide suggests taking turns sorting objects by size, shape or colour at this age.

7. **Blanket fort reading den (18 months and up).** Throw a sheet over two chairs or the edge of a bed. Add a torch and a few picture books. The WHO encourages reading and storytelling with a caregiver when young children are sitting still, so this is a cosy way to fill the quiet part of the day.

8. **Rain-window watching (12 months and up).** Sit together by a window and talk about what you see: big drops, small drops, a drop racing another drop down the glass. Name the sounds too. It is free, calm and surprisingly absorbing.

9. **Kitchen helper (2 years and up).** Let your toddler wash a few vegetables in a shallow bowl, tear lettuce or stir dry ingredients. Keep knives and hot surfaces well out of reach and stay right beside them. In one of our episodes, Blu "helps" with a bag of flour and turns the whole kitchen, and the Puppy, white. Laying down an old sheet first saves a lot of sweeping.

10. **Follow the leader (2 years and up).** Take turns being the leader: hop, crawl, tiptoe, roar. Harvard's guide lists simple imitation games like this as good practice for older toddlers' memory and attention.

## Mixing active and calm

A rhythm that works for many families on a long wet day:

- Morning: one active game (cushion path, freeze dance), then one calm one (sorting basket).
- Before lunch: kitchen helper.
- After nap: tape road or sock-ball toss, then the reading den.
- Late afternoon: window watching while dinner cooks.

You do not need to fill every minute. Boredom for a few minutes often turns into your child's own game, and that is worth protecting.

If you need a stretch of calm so you can cook or answer a call, our list of [quiet-time activities for when you need 10 minutes](/blog/quiet-time-activities-toddlers/) has ideas that toddlers can mostly do on their own nearby.

## Where screens fit

We make a show for small children, so we will be honest: screens can be part of a balanced rainy day. The WHO advises no sedentary screen time for children under 2 and no more than one hour a day for 2- to 4-year-olds, with less being better. The American Academy of Pediatrics suggests using screens at set times rather than whenever a child is bored or upset, and watching together and talking about what you saw. A planned show after lunch, followed by acting out a favourite scene, fits easily alongside the ideas above.

## Safety notes for indoor play

- **Small objects.** The American Academy of Pediatrics advises avoiding toys and objects that can fit entirely in a young child's mouth. Coins, button batteries, marbles, pen caps and small toy parts are common hazards. Check the floor before and after play.
- **Balloons.** HealthyChildren.org notes that balloons remain a choking risk up to age 8. Skip them for this age group.
- **Water.** If you use a bowl or tub of water, stay within arm's reach and empty it as soon as you finish.
- **Older siblings.** Big brothers and sisters can hand small things to toddlers, so keep an eye on shared play.
- **Climbing.** Cushion paths and forts tempt climbing. Keep them low and away from windows and shelves.

## A gentle reminder

Rainy days are not a test of how many activities you can set up. One sock-ball game and a long cuddle with a book is a good day. If your home is small, our post on [indoor play ideas for small homes](/blog/indoor-play-small-homes/) has more space-light ideas.
