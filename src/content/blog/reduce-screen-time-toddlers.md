## The short answer

Cut screen time gradually, not overnight. Move screens to set times of day, give warnings before a show ends, have the next activity ready, and keep a few screen-free zones like meals and bedtime. For 2- to 4-year-olds, the WHO suggests no more than an hour a day of sitting-down screen time, and none for babies under 2.

But toddlers learn most from people, play and movement, and that is where most of their day should go.

## What the guidance says

- **World Health Organization:** no sedentary screen time for babies under 1 or for 1-year-olds. For children aged 2 to 4, no more than one hour a day, and "less is better". The same guidelines ask for at least 180 minutes of physical activity a day for 1- to 4-year-olds.
- **American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP):** its 5 C's guidance for toddlers and preschoolers does not set a single number. Instead it asks families to think about their child, the content, how screens are used for calm, what screens crowd out, and how families talk about media. It strongly discourages giving toddlers open access to a tablet or phone.

Both point the same way: fewer, better, shared screens, with plenty of play around them.

## Why turning it off causes a meltdown

A toddler who is pulled away from a show mid-scene is losing something they were enjoying, with no warning and no idea what comes next. That is a big feeling for a small person. Reducing screen time without a fight is mostly about making that moment easier.

## 9 ways to cut back without a battle

1. **Watch at set times.** The AAP suggests using screens at regular times rather than on demand. If the show is always after lunch, your toddler stops asking at breakfast. Predictability does a lot of the work.

2. **Choose episodes, not endless feeds.** Pick one or two episodes in advance. The AAP recommends turning off autoplay, so the next video does not start by itself and the ending is clear.

3. **Give a warning.** "Two more minutes, then the show says bye-bye." A sand timer or a song can help younger toddlers who do not understand minutes yet.

4. **Let the episode finish.** Stopping at the natural end of a story is far easier than cutting it off halfway.

5. **Have the next thing ready.** The moment the screen goes off, offer something concrete: "Now let's find the puppy from the show in your toy box." Moving straight into play gives the feeling somewhere to go.

6. **Name the feeling.** "You are sad it finished. You really liked that." You do not need to fix it. Naming it and staying close is often enough.

7. **Keep screen-free zones.** The AAP's family media plan suggests device-free times and places, such as meals and bedtime. Starting with just one, like no screens at the dinner table, is a realistic first step.

8. **Find other ways to calm.** The AAP asks parents to try not to hand over a device every time a toddler is upset or bored. Build a few calm-down habits instead: a cuddle, a book, a quiet song, water play in the sink. This takes practice for everyone.

9. **Look at your own screen too.** Toddlers copy. Putting your phone in another room during play time makes it easier for them, and for you.

## Make screen time shared time

The AAP encourages parents to talk with children about what they watch and to do activities related to the show. A few ideas:

- Pause and ask, "How do you think he feels?"
- Act out a favourite scene afterwards.
- Draw or colour the characters.

Our own episodes have no dialogue, which leaves room for exactly this kind of talk. When Blu hugs Mum's tummy before his baby sister arrives, a parent can ask, "What is Blu thinking about?" and let a toddler answer in their own way.

## What to do with the time you win back

The AAP's general 5 C's guidance suggests focusing less on cutting screen time and more on what your family wants to gain back, such as sleep, family time or outdoor play. That shift helps. You are not taking something away; you are making room.

## A sample week of cutting back

- **Days 1 to 3:** keep the total the same, but move screens to two fixed times. Turn off autoplay.
- **Days 4 to 7:** drop one of the two times. Fill it with a simple play activity you have set up in advance.
- **Week 2:** add one screen-free zone, such as meals.
- **Week 3:** watch together at least once a day and talk about it.

Go slower if your child needs it. Small, steady changes stick better than a big rule announced on a hard day.

## Be kind to yourself

There will be days when a show buys you the time to cook, rest or look after a sick sibling. That is normal family life. One day does not undo your habits. Aim for balance across the week, not perfection every afternoon.
